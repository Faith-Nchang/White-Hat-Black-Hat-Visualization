Black hat write-up

1. Selective filtering. We show only data from 2007 to 2020, leaving out earlier and later years. This narrow time window emphasizes the decline from the 2007 peak to the 2020 low, while hiding the broader context: emissions fell by only around 2.4% over the full 1990–2022 period. Ending the chart in 2020 also highlights a year when COVID-19 restrictions temporarily reduced economic activity, transportation, and industrial operations. Without the later data, viewers cannot tell whether the decline continued.

2. Truncated y-axis. We narrow the vertical axis range, which makes the 2007–2020 drop look more dramatic than it would on a scale starting at zero. This visual emphasis can lead viewers to overestimate the size of the change.

3. Misleading title. By omitting the chart's date range and presenting the decline as a current trend, the title may lead viewers to assume emissions are still falling at the same rate. The data stop at 2020, so they do not support that conclusion.
